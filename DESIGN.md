# Design

## Purpose and Current Status

`Venue` represents the physical location and its public information, photos, and Instagram. A venue has multiple recurring `Program` records, each with its own weekdays/start time, time zone, pay, private instructions, booking rules, participant roster, invitations, and calendar. `Booking` represents one dated reservation; this is not a separate dated-event resource. Participants are users with the program-scoped `participant` membership role, while `program_coordinator` grants organizer access. Authentication sessions remain separate from `Program`.

Each program has audited, editable singular `program_label` and `participant_label` settings, defaulting to Program and Participant. Labels apply to its settings, calendar, roster, booking controls, invitations, and messages; Pay is fixed. Labels affect presentation only: changing them never changes roles, booking limits, gender requirements, invitation cycles, or privacy. There are no presets, venue defaults, attendee reservations, or configurable capacity rules.

Deployment startup creates declared MongoDB indexes before serving requests. Historical schema conversions, venue splits, Instagram data conversion, and slug backfills have been retired; only the current schema is supported. The sole instance must complete the conversion supplied by PR #52 before deploying this version. Normal saves generate and retain slugs; normal profile input still accepts Instagram handles or URLs. No startup data rewrites or migration flags remain.

Global admins manage everything and assign venue coordinators. Venue coordinators edit their location, create/manage every program there, and assign program coordinators. Program coordinators manage settings, participants, invitations, and bookings only for assigned programs, without assigning peers or parent roles. Participants have independent access and one upcoming booking per program, with no additional cross-program conflict constraint. Public locations and calendars never grant private access.

The signed-in homepage retains upcoming bookings, then lists programs with an unused active participant booking invitation and no current booking, followed by programs the account manages. The booking list reuses Profile's access snapshots and card partial, including future first bookable dates, while excluding paused, exhausted, revoked, and inactive access. Management uses the existing program management scope, including inherited venue coordination and inactive locations, without exposing unassigned siblings. System admins see all programs they administer; mixed-role accounts see both lists with distinct Choose a Date and Manage Calendar actions. Empty homepage lists are hidden, and card photos and parent venues are batch loaded.

Posebook coordinates invitation-first participant registration and scheduling across recurring programs. Programs specialize their presentation through configurable labels without introducing domain-specific account types.

The application currently provides first-user registration, role-bearing user accounts, email/password login, email password recovery, account settings, deactivation/reactivation, program configuration, scoped organizer/participant access, personal participant invitations, calendar booking with saved times and dated schedule exceptions, retained booking history, and a Rails `/up` liveness endpoint. `/up` proves application boot, not MongoDB connectivity. Automated reminders are not implemented yet.

Public `/about`, `/privacy`, and `/terms` pages are linked from the shared footer, which displays a current-year copyright notice for Vestris LLC. Copyright and information links share one row when space permits and wrap together on narrow screens. It stays at the bottom of the browser window on short pages and follows longer content without covering it. About identifies Vestris LLC. as the operator and links to its website, the source code, issue tracker, license, policies, and public support contact. The generic policy drafts name Vestris LLC. and describe current data handling, including private/public identity, retained history, cookies, and optional Google Calendar access and sharing. They do not introduce an acceptance checkbox or change registration or booking permissions. Operators should obtain legal review before relying on the drafts.

The shared header pairs a serif Posebook wordmark with an original AI-generated sanguine portrait supplied by @dblock: a tightly cropped woman's face with natural proportions and detailed pencil shading. Transparent PNG assets supply the 64-pixel-square header image and a separate 64-pixel-square favicon through the local asset pipeline. The visible wordmark names the home link, and the image is decorative for screen readers. No external font or image service is required.

The artwork is covered by the project's MIT license. Preserve the supplied raster unchanged in `artwork/posebook-logo-source.png`; it is not made of editable vector paths. The source contains neutral checkerboard pixels as well as transparency. Display assets recover the pencil strokes from their red-versus-blue color difference and render them as translucent sanguine ink, removing the checkerboard without filling the portrait's highlights. Regenerate the display assets with ImageMagick:

```sh
magick artwork/posebook-logo-source.png -channel A -fx 'a*max(0,min(1,(r-b-0.008)/0.42))' +channel -channel RGB -fill '#a34c33' -colorize 100 +channel -resize 256x256 -strip app/assets/images/posebook-logo.png
magick app/assets/images/posebook-logo.png -resize 64x64 -gravity center -background none -extent 64x64 -strip app/assets/images/posebook-favicon.png
```

Inspect the full portrait, the header at its actual size, and the favicon at 16 and 32 pixels after artwork changes. Keep source artwork outside the public asset pipeline.

The interface uses the official daisyUI 5.8.0 Caramellatte theme, with cream surfaces, earthy brown text, rounded fields, serif headings, and sanguine primary controls matching the logo. Its MIT-licensed theme CSS is stored locally with its license and source URL; semantic application styles use those variables without a Tailwind compiler, Node dependency, runtime CDN, or external fonts. The theme stays light regardless of the device's dark-mode preference. Calendars retain their existing pale-green/darker-green/gray meaning. Calendars and wide tables scroll inside labeled, keyboard-accessible regions on narrow screens rather than breaking status words into tiny columns.

Picture-bearing lists keep their image space when no photo is available, using local cream-and-sanguine venue, program, and profile SVG placeholders. Venue and program cards, home/profile upcoming bookings, admin users, participant rosters, and manager booking history share this behavior. Program cards still prefer their own photo, then the venue photo, before the placeholder. Placeholder links open the existing venue, program, or participant page; they do not masquerade as uploaded photos or open the full-image dialog. Photo galleries and primary-photo selectors continue to list only real uploads, and public identity/privacy rules are unchanged.

Profile, venue, program, directory, and admin user pages share top tabs and hierarchical breadcrumbs, with the current page marked for screen readers. Information tabs precede a decorative vertical separator and scoped editing/configuration tabs; no separator appears without both groups. Groups wrap on narrow screens. Venues is the public top-level directory link, and Users appears in the header only for system admins. The signed-in account name opens Profile. The venue directory contains only venue cards with their primary photo, address, Maps link, and Instagram, with Create Venue separated for admins. Each venue provides View before the separator, then Programs, Photos, Edit, permitted coordinator management, and Create Program last. Its public Programs tab lists only programs there, without repeating the parent venue card. Programs provide public View and Calendar tabs, plus manager-only History; Photos, the manager-only participant roster, participant invitations, Applications, Edit, permitted coordinator management, and Google Calendar are grouped after the separator, with the external integration last. Public Programs and Photos tabs retain their existing read access without granting editing permissions. Model history is reached through Profile, not program tabs; legacy scoped program-history URLs remain private and usable. Profile View lists current bookings and programs with an unused, active booking invitation and no current booking; paused, exhausted, non-participant, and revoked memberships cannot gain booking access. Profile History retains the signed-in user's records across all programs, including canceled/completed bookings and lost memberships, without exposing other users or manager-only override/pause reasons. Account Details is a separate owner-only read page without photos; successful profile saves and edit cancellation return there. Photos retains its dedicated authorized gallery and joins Edit and Security after the profile's separator. Tabbed pages keep full content width and server-side authorization.

Form submissions immediately disable action buttons, show them pale and busy, and prevent repeat submissions until navigation completes. A temporary hidden field preserves the selected button's name/value, including calendar buttons outside their form; ordinary fields remain enabled so their values and uploads are preserved. Browser validation does not lock invalid forms, canceled submissions recover, and returning with browser Back or Forward restores only controls disabled by this feedback and removes the temporary field. Button-styled navigation links use the same pale, disabled feedback, while modified/new-tab clicks retain native behavior. Copy buttons disable only while the clipboard operation is pending, then recover on success or failure. Forms continue working without JavaScript.

Displayed states share compact, single-word status labels: green for positive states, sanguine for canceled/revoked/inactive states, amber for booked/pending/paused/attention states, and warm cream for neutral states. Booking, calendar, invitation, roster, account, schedule, and photo-visibility displays use the same helper and styles. Each booking presentation has one label: Canceled or Rescheduled takes precedence, then Completed for an expired reservation, otherwise Confirmed for acknowledged attendance or Booked. The organizer's attendance selector uses Booked for attendance not yet confirmed. Private overdue follow-up remains plain text, not a second label. Roster rows show one booking/access label, with retained bookings described as plain text for paused/inactive participants. Access labels use Invited or Uninvited; Inactive, Pending, and Regular labels keep longer explanations outside the badge. Color never replaces visible status text; retained audit explanations and form options remain ordinary text.

Roster pages batch booking and completion/grant history for only the displayed memberships, using request-local read snapshots rather than per-row queries. Mutation services continue to check current database state inside their existing transactions. Venue photos load once per rendered page. Calendar navigation requests send their current access context; only a match against the server's freshly authorized context returns a calendar fragment. Changed roles, accounts, schedule revisions, or attendance windows return the full page so the browser replaces stale private controls and summaries. The response varies by the context header; no shared cache of private HTML or booking permission is introduced.

Profile/home dashboards batch the signed-in participant's booking-access history, parent venues, organizer contacts, and management audience checks. Directory/program cards batch owned primary-photo preferences and first-photo fallback IDs without loading entire galleries; single-owner reads fetch only the chosen or first photo. These are request-local read snapshots, invalidated on owner reload or preference changes, never persistent permission/image caches. Calendar gender previews reuse booked and empty dates already loaded for the displayed range, fetching missing adjacent dates outside it; booking mutations still query live state. Private profile history uses indexed user-scoped queries, eager parent/previous-booking reads, and stable 50-record pages with Previous/Next links. No records or upload capacity are removed.

Each calendar instance caches successful per-date schedule start and end calculations, including missing times, for that render only. A new calendar reads the current schedule, and booking mutations continue to validate against fresh transactional state. Daylight-saving errors retain their existing handling.

Program View is photo-free and summarizes the next future scheduled date within the program's local booking window, including dated openings, closures, overnight end times, and daylight-saving validity. It shows the date, actual time range, time zone, and only the booked participant's public identity and consented Instagram; missing, private, or inactive identities remain To be announced even for managers in this public summary. Canceled/foreign bookings are excluded, and an empty schedule has an explicit message. The calendar link opens the selected date's month. Venue information and existing member-only instructions, pay, and personal booking controls remain available; uploaded photos stay in their dedicated tabs.

Compact Copy controls appear inside read-only private invitation link fields, for both participant and coordinator invitations. Venue and program View pages instead provide Copy Link buttons for their canonical absolute URLs, without query strings. Account/contact details, Instagram handles, and venue addresses retain their text and links without copy buttons. Successful buttons display Copied for three seconds before reverting to their original Copy or Copy Link label, with screen-reader confirmation; failures show manual-copy instructions and expose the public URL for manual copying when there is no invitation field. Never recover single-use invitation tokens on later visits. Use the browser clipboard API with a selection-based fallback when it is unavailable. Controls are hidden without JavaScript, leaving read-only invitation fields or ordinary public URL links available for manual copying. No inline scripts or external services are required.

Roster Actions contains compact Pause/Resume controls; individual Invite to Book remains in Booking Access. Linked model identity and thumbnails open the existing participant page without a redundant View Photos action. Profile, venue, and program photo editors center primary labels and compact primary-selection buttons beneath each photo. Venue and program galleries also center Delete Photo; their primary labels and selection buttons share a minimum-height row with equal spacing above and below, aligning deletion controls across adjacent photos.

Private participant selectors, roster/history contact links, and participant invitation lists share the `Name <email> (Gender)` format. Missing name or gender is omitted independently, leaving just the email when neither is available; name-only and unrestricted personal invitations retain their available name or Personal invitation fallback. Gender appears when stored on the displayed record, regardless of booking rules. Invitation labels never look up an unrelated account by email. Instagram remains a separate roster/history link, not part of selector labels; public identities and name-only admin cells are unchanged.

Personal registration invitations have a separate manager-only tab named with the singular participant label, such as Model Invitations. Its private, non-cached table shows the recipient, status, link creation time, sender name, expiry, acceptance time, and compact View/Revoke actions; only Pending invitations offer Revoke. Times use the program's time zone, and creation does not imply email delivery. Stable newest-first pages contain at most 50 invitations and retain access to older history. Creation, detail, and revocation navigation stays in this tab, while the participant roster retains its separate Invite to Book controls.

Assigned program coordinators, inherited venue coordinators, and system admins can edit a participant's account name and public display name through the program roster. Both names are account-wide; the existing public-name fallback and Private Identity override remain unchanged. Coordinator notes belong to the participant's program membership, are plain text without an application length cap, and are visible only to that program's managers, never participants, other programs, public pages, or calendar providers. Blank notes clear the field. Name and note updates validate and commit together with attributed history, reloading the actor and scoped membership inside the transaction; unrelated account, privacy, role, booking, and invitation fields are not editable here. These edits do not change schedule revisions or booking permissions.

The participant editor shows saved account data separately from the editable draft and a public-card preview. Current contacts, private primary photo, gender, saved display name, and sharing preferences remain read-only. The preview includes only the public name, consented Instagram, and public photos; Private Identity and inactive accounts remain anonymous. A small page-specific script updates the name with text content while typing, using the account name when the display name is blank, without network requests or automatic saves. Server-rendered previews also work without JavaScript and retain draft names after validation errors while saved account data stays unchanged.

The admin user directory shows a linked, local placeholder portrait when an account has no profile photo, keeping identities aligned with photo-bearing rows. Deactivate and Reactivate use the same compact controls as roster actions; protected administrator accounts retain their non-interactive label.

Single-item breadcrumbs have a decorative leading `>`; longer trails retain `/` separators. Both markers are hidden from screen readers.

All table buttons, submit inputs, button-styled links, and secondary photo controls use one compact size: `0.75rem` text, a `1.75rem` minimum height, and `0.25rem` by `0.5rem` padding. This includes user activation, participant invitation/pause/resume, calendar Book/Choose actions, and profile/venue/program Delete Photo and Set Primary Photo controls. Profile photo visibility controls share that size. Width follows the label instead of filling a cell or photo column; photo actions wrap without squeezing their labels into fixed half-width forms. Upload buttons, full-image dialog controls, standalone forms, bulk actions, plain links, and disclosure summaries retain their existing styles.

Participants' upcoming bookings use the same bordered card on the homepage and program View/Calendar pages, grouping the saved date/time, attendance state, confirmation, and reschedule/cancel buttons. The homepage includes the venue's public primary photo, name, address, and Google Maps link; bookings are scoped to the signed-in participant's memberships and sorted by saved start time. Paused participants retain their bookings and cancellation/confirmation controls without gaining rescheduling rights. Venue coordinators and system admins select an audited primary photo in the venue Photos tab; selection never changes public visibility and falls back to the first remaining photo when unset or deleted.

## Participant Applications

The application lifecycle is program-scoped, with intake disabled by default. Verified accounts submit a message and explicit profile-review consent without gaining participant or booking rights. Assigned program coordinators, inherited venue coordinators, and system admins can accept or choose Not Now; a past Not Now decision remains available for later acceptance. Acceptance uses the same membership and initial booking-grant path as invitation acceptance, preserving existing roles, pauses, and consumed grants. Intake closure does not discard existing applications, while inactive programs/accounts cannot be accepted.

`ParticipantApplication` retains current state, a decision revision, and submission-time name/email/gender snapshots. Manager decisions recheck authority and the submitted revision inside a transaction; admission uses the shared program schedule lock. Append-only `ParticipationEvent` records retain application submission, each decision, withdrawal, and invitation acceptance with source, actor, and time. Decision notes remain private to managers. Applicants can withdraw unaccepted applications to prevent later admission. Existing membership origins are not reconstructed or guessed.

Program Edit exposes opt-in intake; public View offers Apply to Participate only while intake and the program are active. Applicants prepare an account first, then explicitly submit review consent and an optional message. Profile Applications lists only the signed-in account's submissions. Managers use a scoped Applications tab with Pending, Not Now, Accepted, Withdrawn, and All filters, revision-bearing Accept/Not Now forms, and Participation History distinguishing application and invitation origins. Lists use stable 50-record pages. Private reviewer notes never reach applicant pages.

The public action and Program Edit share a friendly `/programs/:program_id/apply` entry link, resolving current/retained slugs and IDs through the existing program lookup. Its redirect prohibits caching and reuses the normal intake, active-parent, verified-account, duplicate-application, and participant checks; it never submits an application. Intake remains independently controlled by each recurring program, not individual dated bookings. Closing intake makes the entry link unavailable without removing owner status/history.

Both personal invitation acceptance and application acceptance enqueue separate admission alerts for active assigned program/venue coordinators, sending one per person across dual assignments and not subscribing global administrators automatically. An accepted applicant still receives their distinct decision email, even if they are also a coordinator. The token-free `participation_admission` outbox and ID-only job join the existing admission transaction, so enqueue failure rolls back invitation consumption, account creation, membership, and history. Private invitation acceptance remains available without configured email or an assigned recipient; applications retain their existing email prerequisite. Workers bind the accepted source to its program and participant, then recheck active accounts, current participant membership, active program/venue, and recipient assignments. Messages identify the acceptance source and participant name, never contacts or private reviewer notes, and link to authenticated participation history with a scoped sign-in return. Retried jobs retain the existing at-least-once SMTP delivery limitation.

Pending and Not Now applications with recorded consent permit the assigned program/venue coordinators to read the applicant's current private contact/profile/photo information, never edit photos. Every image request rechecks access. Withdrawal ends application-only access, including direct thumbnails, while retained submission snapshots/history remain available and later identity changes stay private. Acceptance uses ordinary participant-organizer access instead. Existing participant membership is not revoked by withdrawal.

Active, opt-in programs provide email verification before application account registration. `ApplicationRegistration` stores a normalized recipient and a hashed, single-use, 30-minute token, with eventual TTL cleanup. The encrypted email outbox queues ID-only jobs; workers discard stale links when intake closes. GET never consumes a link. An explicit matching-account confirmation or valid new-account form atomically consumes it and records email verification, preserving existing roles and privacy or creating an ordinary account without program access. Wrong-account logout/login preserves the verification flow. Normal public registration stays closed; no private-photo review access is granted by email verification alone.

Submission queues one separate email for each active assigned program/venue coordinator, combining duplicate assignments without sharing recipient lists. System admins are not automatically subscribed; assign them a coordinator role if they should receive these emails. Submission requires configured email and at least one active assigned coordinator. Acceptance and courteous Not Now decisions queue an applicant email without reviewer notes. Durable outbox records and ID-only jobs are written inside the existing workflow transaction through `EmailDelivery.enqueue_in_transaction`; failed enqueue rolls back application/decision/history/admission and reports an error without losing the submission draft. No SMTP operation runs inside a transaction.

Workers recheck the event's source program/applicant, current status, active recipient, and current coordinator assignment before building mail. Withdrawn or superseded submission/decision messages are discarded; acceptance notices also require retained participation, and applicant notices require a verified current address. Authenticated email links preserve the review/status destination through sign-in and never decide by GET. Existing worker retries apply. SMTP acknowledgment failures can duplicate a delivered message, and an already in-flight message cannot be recalled; current application status is authoritative. Booking reminders remain unimplemented.

## Google Calendar Synchronization

Programs configure whole-number `calendar_invitation_lead_minutes` of 0 or greater, defaulting to 0 for new and existing records. Only Google event starts move earlier by that many elapsed minutes. Saved session times, event ends, booking history, completion, and attendance deadlines stay unchanged; the no-end-time fallback remains one hour after the actual session start. Earlier invitations include the actual session start in their description. Audited program-setting changes queue all upcoming bookings, including dated overrides, through the existing transaction and outbox, without calling Google before commit.

Optional Google OAuth connects a private calendar per program. Only booked slots are mirrored, with the model's private contact details and effective Instagram tagging preferences; invitations go to the model and authorized program/venue organizers. Rescheduling moves the original event, organizer replacements update its model, schedule edits update saved times, and cancellations cancel the Google event. Google changes never mutate Posebook bookings. Read-only model RSVP checks run every 12 hours through individual shared-worker jobs; response snapshots stay in the calendar outbox, tied to the current booking, destination calendar, and synchronized payload. Authorized organizers see separate Pending, Accepted, Tentative, or Declined labels with a last-checked tooltip. Google acceptance never confirms Posebook attendance, and decline never cancels a booking. Missing events or attendees clear the response; provider errors retain the last successful snapshot and retry without storing private error text. Missing session end times use a one-hour Google-only duration. Default Google reminders and guest modification/invitation permissions are disabled.

The official Google Calendar Ruby client manages provider calls outside booking transactions. A calendar-specific MongoDB outbox is updated inside those transactions, coalesces each booking replacement chain, uses deterministic Google event IDs, leases worker claims, and retries errors without retaining private response text. Recurring Active Jobs in the shared worker drain due changes and reconcile upcoming bookings every five minutes, skipping unchanged payloads. The outbox retains calendar state separately from the generic job queue. Refresh tokens are encrypted with the application secret, never audited or displayed. OAuth callbacks bind single-use expiring state to the signed-in manager and recheck program scope. Connecting organizers must retain active management access. Disconnect clears credentials without deleting external events. See [Google Calendar Setup](docs/google-calendar.md).

Each booking creates one shared Google meeting with the model and organizers on a visible guest list, including their email addresses. The event Location contains the venue address. Event privacy and disabled guest edits/invitations remain unchanged; guest visibility applies only to invitees, not a public roster. Google can display linked invitation copies in personal calendars. Periodic reconciliation updates existing upcoming meetings in place when guest visibility changes.

## Accounts and Recovery

There is one `User` model, not a separate admin account type. A blank installation allows anyone to register the first user without an installation secret, as explicitly requested. That account receives `system_admin` in its global roles. Public registration closes once any user exists; subsequent accounts require a personal invitation or email-verified account preparation for an active, opt-in program. A fixed bootstrap document ID uses MongoDB's built-in unique `_id` constraint to prevent two concurrent first-user claims, without requiring a transaction or replica set.

Use email/password authentication initially, with BCrypt password hashing, a 12-character minimum, and the library's 72-byte maximum. Passwordless access is deferred. Public forms never accept role assignment. Sessions use encrypted, HTTP-only, same-site cookies, rotate on login/logout, and include a per-user session version. Ordinary sessions last seven days. Sign-in offers an unchecked "Remember me" choice for trusted devices: a separate encrypted credential has no application deadline and restores a fresh session after the ordinary session expires. Its browser cookie requests twenty years of storage, renewed on visits; browsers may cap that lifetime or clear storage, so literal forever persistence cannot be guaranteed. Encryption metadata deliberately has no expiry; browser storage expiry is applied separately using public cookie APIs. The credential contains only the account ID and current session version, never a password.

Failed login never grants a remembered credential. Logout or an unchecked sign-in clears it. Password changes revoke every older session/remembered credential and clear the current browser's remembered choice, retaining only an ordinary seven-day session there. Confirmed email changes, immediate administrator email changes, password resets, and account deactivation revoke remembered credentials through the same account/session checks. Merely requesting an unconfirmed email change does not revoke them. Existing-account invitation acceptance preserves the choice while rotating the session; new registrations use the ordinary lifetime. Credentials are checked against active account state on every restoration, and cookie protections remain HTTP-only, same-site, and restricted to HTTPS in production.

Email recovery links use random tokens, store only their SHA-256 digests, expire after 30 minutes, and are consumed atomically with the password update. Resetting rotates the session version, invalidating every existing session. A newer reset request supersedes older links. Requests use the same success message for known and unknown emails. Login, signup, and reset requests use Rails rate limits backed by the application's cache; these limits are per process with the current memory cache, not distributed across instances.

Account settings allow name and required phone changes. Phone input accepts spaces, parentheses, periods, and dashes, with optional `+` or `00` for country codes; ten-digit numbers without a country code are treated as US/Canada. Numbers are stored with a leading `+` and digits only. Phone numbers are unverified and cannot be used for recovery or messaging yet. Email changes require the current password and a single-use, 30-minute verification link at the new address. GET displays confirmation without changing the address; an atomic PATCH consumes the token, changes email under its unique index, invalidates all sessions, and clears authentication challenges. Until then, login and recovery use the old email. Authenticated password changes require the current password, rotate session versions, retain the current browser, and invalidate other sessions and outstanding challenges.

The profile View tab is a read-only account summary. Edit has its own form; Security groups password changes and account deactivation without combining their submissions. Existing password/deactivation page URLs redirect to the matching Security section. Successful profile/password edits redirect back to the summary with a confirmation; invalid submissions retain the appropriate form and active tab. Edit and Security have a cancel link to the summary. Successful deactivation signs out the user and confirms the result on the sign-in page rather than returning to an authenticated page; system admins remain protected and see no deactivation form.

Registration and profile editing collect an optional public display name, an Instagram handle, and a tagging preference. First-user and invitation registration forms default tagging on; a valid handle is required whenever it is selected. Existing opt-outs remain unchanged. For a new private profile whose disabled sharing fields are not submitted, tagging remains off until explicitly enabled. `public_name` falls back to the account name when the display name is blank; forms explain that either will be public in announcements and session calendars. Handle and profile-URL inputs normalize to lowercase handles without `@`; only `instagram_handle` is stored. Other hosts, content URLs, credentials, and nonstandard ports are rejected. Display uses `@handle` and derived HTTPS profile links. `public_instagram_url` and `public_instagram_handle` return nothing when tagging is off or the profile is private. System admins can edit display names, handles, tagging, and privacy preferences. Changing a handle preserves the selected tagging preference; concurrent saves keep the chosen handle and tagging setting together. Preferences are global across sessions, do not verify Instagram ownership, and do not add announcements or a public directory.

Users may choose `public_profile_private` during registration or profile editing, and system admins may edit it too. It defaults off to preserve existing public preferences. When private, `public_name` returns "To be announced" and both public Instagram helpers return nothing, regardless of saved display-name/tagging choices. The global switch applies at every program and hides identity from guests and other participants, not authorized program managers. Managers retain the account name in their private calendar and booking history. Native disabled fieldsets and a small JavaScript enhancement disable/dim sharing controls without erasing stored values; turning privacy off restores those preferences. The server enforces privacy independently of disabled controls. Edits that omit the preference preserve concurrent privacy changes.

Each account has a photo gallery with no application photo-count cap, stored in the same MongoDB database using `mongoid-grid_fs`. Owners and system admins upload, delete, and independently mark each photo Public or Private; new uploads are private. Assigned program organizers may view their participants' private photos but cannot change them. Public galleries expose only selected public photos and the current public name; Private Identity or account inactivity hides the gallery and every image publicly without erasing sharing preferences. Calendar participant names link to available galleries, and the private participant roster links organizers to photos. No public participant directory exists.

Owners and system admins choose a primary photo on the account edit page. Selection is an atomic, owner-scoped photo preference; it does not change public visibility. Private model tables show the chosen thumbnail, or the first remaining photo when no selection exists or the chosen photo is deleted, together with linked account name/email and Instagram. Organizer booking selectors include name, email, Instagram, and gender when needed for configured booking rules. The linked participant page shows contact details only to its existing owner/admin/assigned-organizer audience, even when no photos exist; public galleries retain public-name-only identity and effective public-photo filtering.

Uploads accept actual JPEG, PNG, or WebP content up to 10 MB and 40 megapixels. libvips corrects orientation, strips metadata, and creates a full-proportion WebP image bounded to 1600 pixels plus a 400-pixel square thumbnail. Original uploads are not retained. Account profiles show existing photos with their visibility labels. Square thumbnails throughout account, participant, venue, and program galleries open the full image in a shared modal dialog, with keyboard focus containment and Close, Escape, or backdrop dismissal. Closing clears the displayed image; failed image reads show an unavailable message. Without JavaScript, links open the image in the same tab. All image requests, including thumbnails, check current authorization and prohibit browser/proxy caching. An atomic user-document append preserves simultaneous profile uploads without a count cap; rejected/failed uploads clean up their GridFS files, and deletion removes both variants. Account deactivation retains private photo storage. Include the `photos.files` and `photos.chunks` collections in database backups; no persistent local disk or additional storage credentials are needed.

Accounts require a private gender selection of male, female, or other, editable during registration, in the profile, or by a system admin. The one-time migration fills missing genders with male and missing phone numbers with a fictional placeholder; see [Upgrading](UPGRADING.md). Existing reservations, cancellation, and attendance controls remain available. Gender is audited and filtered from request logs, never shown on public pages or other participants' calendars. Organizer booking selectors show gender only when the program has gender rules.

Booking rules implement `evaluate(date, user)`: return `nil` when allowed, otherwise an immutable `BookingRuleDecision` with a stable symbolic `code`, localized `message`, and boolean `overridable`. The gender rule reports `gender_required`, which cannot be overridden, and `gender_restricted`, which permits organizer overrides. Calendars show rule-blocked dates without displaying the gender rule; participant mutations reject every decision, while organizer mutations require the rule's permission and explicit consent with a retained explanation. The rule owns that policy, not consumers inspecting profile fields or message text. Booking mutations still evaluate rules inside the shared schedule transaction. This contract introduces no rule registry, executable expressions, new settings, or stored-schema changes.

Deactivation retains the user and history while revoking sessions, email-change links, and recovery links. Inactive users cannot log in or request recovery; old documents without an active flag remain active. Public identity helpers themselves mask inactive accounts' names and Instagram, alongside the existing photo and calendar authorization checks, without erasing saved sharing preferences. Only system admins can manage other users or reactivate accounts. System admin accounts cannot be deactivated until global role transfer exists: protecting these accounts atomically avoids concurrent last-admin races. Public registration remains closed even if all non-admin accounts are inactive.

Security explains password-confirmed self-deactivation rather than self-service account deletion: all devices lose access, public sharing stops, and account/photo/membership/booking records remain private to their existing authorized audiences. The confirmation repeats the public-sharing and retention result. Existing bookings and private calendar synchronization are not canceled, and previously sent information cannot be recalled; participants are directed to arrange upcoming commitments with their organizer. Reactivation restores saved sharing preferences. The Privacy Policy documents these boundaries and retains the contact route for privacy and deletion requests rather than promising erasure or absolute non-disclosure.

Account deactivation/reactivation uses fresh transactional saves with audit callbacks and session revocation, without requiring unrelated profile fields to pass current validation. Existing incomplete profile values remain unchanged; normal profile and email changes still validate. Failed admin access updates always show a clear error.

Development emails are written to `tmp/mail`; tests use the in-memory mail adapter. Production account email uses configurable SMTP, requires `SMTP_ADDRESS` and `MAIL_FROM`, and reports unavailable delivery rather than pretending to send email. Links use configured mailer hosts, not incoming request hosts. Phone recovery remains future work.

System admins can edit any existing user's name, email, and phone, including inactive accounts, without changing roles, passwords, or active status. Successful edits return to the user list with confirmation; invalid edits stay on the form. Admin email changes apply immediately without verification or the target user's password, clear previous verification, revoke all sessions and outstanding authentication challenges, and handle competing unique email claims. Name/phone-only edits preserve sessions and pending email changes. An admin changing their own email is signed out with confirmation and must sign in again. Account access still has separate explicit controls; missing edits or invalid access values never default to deactivation.

## Venue Photos and Instagram

Venues and programs default to active, including existing documents without a stored flag. Only system admins toggle venues; parent venue coordinators and system admins toggle programs, never program-only coordinators. Audited activation changes reload the location and actor inside a transaction that retries conflicts, without validating unrelated legacy settings. An inactive parent blocks every child without changing its independent active flag. The shared schedule lock writes the venue before the program, serializing activation with new bookings and date changes. Program activation also advances its schedule revision. Inactive locations and image reads are hidden publicly but remain available to authorized managers and upcoming booked participants; private membership scopes remain unchanged for retained cancellations, confirmations, and history. Dashboard booking eligibility and calendar navigation contexts include effective activation state. Existing bookings and calendar invitations are not canceled or deleted.

Venues publish an optional normalized Instagram account and public photos. Programs have independent public galleries in separate `program_photos` documents, alongside the venue's `venue_photos` documents. Each Photos tab exposes uploads, deletion, and primary selection only to the relevant managers; program coordinators cannot change the parent venue or sibling galleries. Program primary selection uses the audited schedule-lock settings service without changing booking times. Unset/deleted selections fall back to the first remaining photo; program cards use the venue primary photo if the program has none. All galleries have no application count cap, while processing, byte/dimension safety, GridFS storage, no-cache responses, and user-gallery privacy remain intact.

Venues also expose a Google Maps link, using an optional validated Google Maps HTTPS place/share URL or an address-based search when unset. Only existing venue editors change it, and the change is audited. Public venue/program pages link to it without embedding a map or requesting location services. Google invitations retain the address in their Location field and include the venue name, address, and Maps link in the description. Existing upcoming meetings receive venue edits through the normal five-minute reconciliation and payload fingerprint, preserving event IDs and booking state; no geocoding API, new credential, or server-side URL fetch is introduced.

The venue Instagram handle is audited with other venue settings. Photo metadata and image bytes are not part of the user/venue/program field audit; failed uploads remove their temporary GridFS files.

## User and Program Audit History

`mongoid-history` stores user and session audit entries in `history_trackers`, including original/modified values, action, version, timestamps, and an optional user modifier. User fields are limited to name, email, phone, display name, Instagram handle/tagging preference, public-identity privacy, global roles, active status, and email verification time. Session history covers name, custom/generated slugs, time zone, start time, participant pay, instructions, booking horizon, and available weekdays. Dated schedule exceptions also retain their configuration changes through the gem. Authentication secrets and challenges, including pending email addresses, password digests, tokens, expiry fields, and session versions, are never tracked.

The authenticated request user supplies the actor; anonymous registration and system/console writes have no actor unless explicitly wrapped in `Current.set(user: actor)`. Actor context is restored after each request and cleared on subsequent system writes. History begins with new writes; existing records receive no fabricated historical baseline. Storage only is implemented: there are no history pages or undo/redo routes.

Email confirmation, deactivation, and reactivation use fresh-document saves inside retrying replica-set transactions, committing the audited change and its history together. Token expiry/single-use and protected-admin predicates are checked again inside each attempt. Password-only operations retain their atomic credential updates without creating history. Other saves use the gem's normal callback semantics, including cleanup after failed persistence; this is not a database-wide audit log. Raw driver writes and atomic updates bypass callbacks and history. Maintenance scripts must use ordinary saves and explicit actor context when audit entries are required.

## Agreed Participant Workflow

An organizer first connects with a participant by email, Instagram, or text. The organizer adds them to the appropriate program, possibly with only a name or social handle, then sends a private registration link by email or through the existing conversation.

The participant invitation flow collects name, email, phone, gender, and password. Personal invitations do not prove ownership of the chosen email address; phone numbers remain unverified. Contact verification and explicit SMS consent must precede any future automated SMS. Declining SMS must not prevent registration or booking.

After accepting an invitation, the participant chooses an available day at the inviting program. The invitation has no preselected date; selection immediately confirms the booking. Participants can cancel or reschedule with an explanation, with confirmation on screen and a retained program history. Booking email/SMS, calendar invitations, and organizer notifications remain planned, not implemented.

One week before participating, send email and consented SMS reminders with a confirmation link. Organizers can see acknowledgments, delivery failures, and upcoming programs. Bookings made within the reminder window receive their booking confirmation immediately, not a redundant overdue reminder.

## Program Boundaries and Scheduling

A participant has one identity and separate program memberships. Organizers see only memberships and booking history for programs they manage. Private contacts and participant rosters remain restricted. Program detail pages are public through `/programs` slugs, retained slugs, and database IDs, without legacy root shortcuts: visitors see only name, address, regular schedule, and the calendar. Confirmed upcoming bookings expose only active participants' current `public_name` and opted-in Instagram helpers. Inactive accounts remain booked without exposing identity. Canceled/rescheduled ledger history, contact information, pay, participant instructions, invitation lists, pause notes, and internal schedule explanations are not public. Closed public dates use a generic explanation; one-off times remain visible without their internal reason.

The visible calendar grid also shows past scheduled programs, including programs without a booked participant. Past non-program days are entirely blank. Retained completed bookings within that grid show their saved time and only the participant's current public name/opted-in Instagram, preserving private identity and inactive-account anonymity; cancellation/rescheduling history, explanations, contacts, and ledger access remain private. Public month navigation still begins at the current program-local month rather than exposing an unrestricted archive.

Venue coordinators inherit access to all programs at their venue through `VenueMembership`. A `ProgramMembership` grants local `program_coordinator` and/or `participant` access, without changing global account roles. Admins invite or promote venue coordinators; admins and venue coordinators invite or promote program coordinators using an email, never a global user directory. Registered active accounts require an explicit management-role confirmation; existing memberships and booking grants are preserved. Unique memberships and atomic role additions/removals preserve concurrent grants and retained records. Revocation removes the specific capability, with any independently granted or inherited access retained.

`CoordinatorInvitation` records grant exactly one venue or program coordinator role, never a system admin or participant role. They use the same hashed, expiring, revocable, single-use token and optional-email policy as participant invitations. A specified recipient email restricts registration and existing-account acceptance to that address; a blank email generates a private bearer link and sends no email. Registration, token consumption, and membership grant commit together in a replica-set transaction, with a fresh existing-account read and normalized email comparison. Wrong signed-in accounts see an explicit logout/account-switch action that preserves the link. The sender must still be active and authorized to delegate the target when the link is accepted. Optional email delivery follows existing invitation delivery configuration; failures retain the private shareable link. Coordinator invitation lists, recipient addresses, and revocation are restricted to system admins for venues and the parent venue's managers for programs.

Program lists and all private management/booking routes retain authenticated, program-scoped authorization; public detail visibility never grants membership or permission. Members retain their own booking information, pay, and participant instructions, while managers retain program history and controls. Schedule access does not permit user administration or organizer role assignment. Only system admins see the global active-user assignment selector; parent venue managers see their program coordinators' emails on scoped management pages. Assigned schedule organizers can invite participants and see their program's invitation/booking history; participants cannot see other participants' private contacts or history. Participants change or cancel only their own upcoming bookings. Invitation-email links still require sign-in and validate membership before returning to the program. Program hostname routing remains future work.

`mongoid-slug` generates unique venue and program slugs during normal saves from the name or an optional custom value. Generated links use `/venues/:slug` and `/programs/:slug`, without root shortcuts or `/sessions` aliases. Nested organizer/invitation/booking routes accept program slugs but remain private and program-scoped. Slug history and original MongoDB IDs continue to resolve, and reserved application paths receive suffixes instead of being shadowed. Blank custom values follow name changes; explicit custom values remain stable. Production startup creates the unique sparse slug indexes without a slug backfill.

Each program stores name, address, IANA time zone, decimal pay in USD, instructions, available weekdays, a regular start time, an optional end time, and a positive booking horizon in months. New programs default to `UTC`, zero pay, Mondays at 9 AM, no end time, and six months. Existing configured values and saved booking instants are not rewritten. Pay is informational, not payment processing.

The usual event times are the program's editable `start_time` and optional `end_time`. An earlier end time means the following day; equal times are invalid. A shared view helper displays regular weekdays, times, nominal duration, and program time zone on program lists, details, calendar headers, management pages, personal invitation pages, and invitation emails. Calendars show each date's actual time and elapsed duration, including exceptions and daylight-saving transitions; booking summaries, cancellation forms, and participant rosters use saved booking times rather than substituting the current usual times.

The server-rendered month calendar uses a program-local, inclusive calendar-month booking horizon and displays each booking's start time. Past dates, unavailable weekdays, closed/occupied days, dates outside the booking window, and additional dates while already scheduled remain unavailable for personal booking. Month navigation is bounded and retains rescheduling context. A dependency-free JavaScript enhancement fetches the page and replaces only the calendar, preserving explanation drafts and supporting browser Back/Forward. A server-derived viewer/access context forces full-page navigation when the user or role changes, including program expiry or membership revocation, so old private information is removed. Superseded requests are canceled, focus moves to the updated month without scrolling, and ordinary links remain the fallback when JavaScript is unavailable or navigation fails. Nonexistent daylight-saving local times are rejected; repeated local times resolve using Rails' daylight-saving preference.

An optional audited `first_bookable_date` limits new bookings and replacements, including organizer scheduling, without redefining when sessions actually run. Its default is blank for existing and new programs. The inclusive booking window starts at the later of that civil date or program-local today, and its end advances by `booking_horizon_months` from that start. A future cutoff sets the default calendar month and displays a notice that invited participants can book later dates now. Explicit navigation still reaches the current local month for existing/manual sessions; public next-session summaries retain earlier real sessions and their exceptions. Dated schedule edits use today through the same horizon end so organizers can maintain earlier sessions and the full extended window. Settings and booking mutations share the existing transactional lock, so stale forms cannot bypass a changed cutoff. Existing bookings, attendance, cancellations, claims, invitations, and historical snapshots are retained.

Calendar display shows only the date for ordinary unscheduled days, "Past" for unbooked past dates, and "Future" beyond the booking horizon, with longer reasons outside the badge. Authorized organizers can select an unscheduled date number to edit it; guests and participants cannot. Leading dates from the prior month show their actual schedule, while guests and participants see a date-only Future placeholder for dates in the following month. Authorized program organizers instead see those following dates' actual schedules and scoped Edit Day/Edit Booking/Schedule Participant controls, still respecting the booking window and server-side authorization. Past dates never offer booking/editing controls; historical schedule exceptions are loaded for the visible grid, while the separate upcoming-booking query continues to govern personal booking permission and summaries.

Each program owns its invitations, roster, recurring weekdays/start time, dated schedule exceptions, time zone, address, pay, instructions, and configurable booking horizon. Default availability is the next six months, opened automatically. System admins edit the regular start time in program settings. Both admins and assigned schedule organizers can manage dated exceptions without gaining general program administration.

`ProgramScheduleException` has a unique program/date index, an open/closed/regular mode, an open-date start time and optional end time, and a required explanation of at most 200 characters. Open overrides weekday availability and both times; closed blocks the date; regular restores the recurring rule without deleting the exception record. `mongoid-history` retains exception versions and actors. Past/out-of-window exception edits are rejected. Closing an occupied booking, including restoring a normally unavailable weekday, cancels its booking with that explanation and releases its claim in the same transaction. Time changes retain the booking and append attributed old/new UTC times. Regular time edits update upcoming regular bookings and their claims but preserve dated overrides; no notification email is sent.

Booking mutations, dated exceptions, and program settings share a transactional program revision write. This serializes overlapping operations and forces conflicting transactions to retry with fresh program/exception state, preventing bookings from slipping through a concurrent closure or retaining a stale start time. Invalid changes and failed claim/time writes roll back settings, exceptions, bookings, and audit entries together. Administrative scripts that change schedules must use these mutation services, not direct updates.

Programs independently select which genders cannot repeat in immediately adjacent scheduled programs; all are unrestricted by default. Both the preceding and following scheduled program are checked, including completed preceding bookings. An unbooked scheduled program breaks the sequence rather than looking farther for a booked participant. Dated openings participate, while closures and nonexistent daylight-saving start times are skipped. Canceled/rescheduled records and other programs do not count, and a personal reschedule excludes the reservation being replaced. The participant's calendar and transactional mutations share the same rule, using current profile genders without publishing them. Later rule, schedule, or gender edits do not automatically cancel existing reservations. Assigned organizers can explicitly override a consecutive-gender conflict with the booking explanation; its reason, creating actor, and creation time remain on the retained replacement/new booking, visible only in manager history. Missing gender selections cannot be overridden.

A participant may have one upcoming booking per program and a day may have one participant. Before creating a booking, participants review and confirm the date and time; the displayed schedule revision is checked within the booking transaction so stale confirmations cannot book changed schedules. `Booking` stores the retained history in `bookings`: original date, program/user, time-zone snapshot, UTC start, optional scheduled end, expiry, time-change entries, status, transition explanation/actor/time, and a previous-booking link on replacements. A configured end determines completion and claim expiry; without one, expiry remains the saved local end of day. Older bookings without a start instant display the original 6:30 PM in their saved time zone, and existing bookings keep their previous expiry until an applicable schedule edit. Cancellation closes the record; rescheduling closes the original and creates a linked replacement. Application deletion entry points are guarded and there is no booking deletion route.

`BookingClaim` contains disposable active pointers, with database-unique program/date and program/user indexes. A replica-set transaction commits each booking, cancellation, or reschedule with its claim update, so a failed replacement cannot lose the original reservation or leave a ledger record. Rescheduling updates the existing claim rather than trying to reuse a deleted unique key inside a transaction. Expired claims are removed before booking mutations; retained past bookings display Completed from their saved UTC expiry. Rollover requires no background job. Historical bookings are never removed by expiry or cancellation, and program setting changes do not rewrite them.

Calculate weekly programs and the one-week reminder in the program's local calendar, then store instants in UTC. Cover daylight saving changes. Cancellation and rescheduling must invalidate obsolete pending messages and update calendar invitations. Reminder delivery requires a durable worker, idempotent scheduling, retries, opt-out handling, and acknowledgment tracking; a successful provider request is not proof of recipient delivery.

Attendance confirmation opens at local midnight seven calendar days before the saved booking date and closes at the booking's saved start instant. It is separate from the reservation's existing `confirmed` status. Participants confirm through **I'll be there!** and cannot clear that confirmation. Before the inclusive five-calendar-day rescheduling cutoff, **I can no longer make it.** opens the existing explanation-and-date workflow. A booking without attendance confirmation at or beyond the cutoff shows an urgent organizer-contact notice to its participant and an overdue warning to authorized managers on the calendar, history, roster, and editor. Positive confirmation is public without exposing private identity; overdue state, contact details, and change history are private. Calendar-day calculations use the booking's saved program time zone, including across daylight saving and later program-settings changes. A program without listed active organizers directs the participant to contact the program. Cancellation remains available and retains the booking history.

`attendance_confirmed_at` and `attendance_confirmed_by_id` store the latest confirmation metadata; append-only `attendance_changes` retain each transition, actor, time, and explanation. The persisted reservation state remains `confirmed` independently of attendance; display combines the two without changing booking or mutation rules. Canceled, rescheduled, and completed bookings show only their terminal label, retaining previous attendance confirmation in the historical explanation rather than another badge. Personal confirmation and organizer mutations use the same retrying program schedule lock as bookings and recheck fresh authorization and booking state inside it. Repeated personal confirmations are idempotent. Date/participant replacements retain the original confirmation history; participant-initiated replacements start without attendance confirmation. Assigned organizers and system admins can create, reassign, reschedule, cancel, and set/clear attendance for upcoming bookings at managed programs, without changing unrelated account or program-settings permissions. They explicitly choose the resulting attendance state, and replacements update the existing unique claim atomically. New bookings retain their creating actor. No attendance email or automated reminder is sent. Calendar navigation reloads the full page if scheduling or attendance context changes, removing stale summaries and controls.

If another participant takes a date after a calendar or organizer form was opened, recheck its claim inside the program schedule transaction and show a friendly date-taken message instead of relying on a duplicate-key error. The participant's refreshed calendar stays on the submitted month; failed rescheduling retains the original reservation and explanation draft. Organizer replacements exclude their original claim so same-date participant reassignment remains possible. Unique indexes remain the final safeguard.

Booking attendance, time, cancellation, and replacement writes use an atomic compare-and-increment `lock_version` alongside their state/history update. Existing records without the field start at revision zero. Participant confirmation/change/cancellation and organizer edit/cancellation forms carry the displayed revision; missing, malformed, or stale revisions cannot mutate a booking. Transactions recheck the original submitted revision on every retry instead of applying stale user intent to newer state. A conflict redirects to the latest program details for review, never a stale form with a refreshed token. Replaced/canceled bookings are detected within authorized scope, so old submissions receive a friendly conflict while foreign bookings remain hidden.

Repeated positive participant confirmations are safe no-ops without duplicate history, but an old confirmation cannot undo a later organizer clear. Schedule-driven time changes also advance booking revisions. Existing program transactions still commit claims, replacement records, cancellation history, attendance changes, and schedule updates together; a failed transaction rolls back its revision increments as well.

## Booking Permission and Participant Pauses

Program participant membership does not automatically grant unlimited repeat bookings. Membership stores the booking invitation time and inviting actor, plus a separate paused flag and short private explanation. The first accepted participant role grants one booking cycle; accepting another registration invitation for an existing participant never renews permission or bypasses a pause. A grant remains usable through cancellation/rescheduling and is exhausted when a confirmed booking completes at its saved expiry. Past-start bookings cannot receive new bookings.

System admins and assigned program organizers manage individual or bulk invitations from the program's Participants page. Bulk delivery selects only active accounts at that program without a pause or upcoming booking. Permission opens immediately. Grants and pause/resume changes share the program schedule transaction lock with booking mutations, and booking/rescheduling rechecks permission inside each transaction attempt. Pauses retain existing bookings and cancellation rights; resume restores only unused permission, never a fresh cycle after completion. Changes are audited through `mongoid-history` on the membership, and pause explanations are not exposed to participants or request logs.

Email is sent once after each successful grant transaction, outside retry blocks. Delivery failures or unavailable SMTP leave access enabled and are reported for explicit follow-up; bulk results report sent, failed, and skipped counts. Email links preserve the intended program across login but resolve it through the signed-in user's authorized program scope. No token grants access by itself, no delayed invitation release is implemented, and no background scheduler is introduced.

The private Participants roster shows 25 participants per page with stable ordering, program-wide counts, and compact invitation/resume controls; pause forms and notes expand on demand. Counts group inactive accounts first, then paused participants, then booked participants, with remaining participants available for invitations, including previously invited participants without upcoming bookings. Bulk invitations always cover every page and recheck availability when granting access. Individual and bulk actions retain the current page.

Legacy memberships without an invitation timestamp may finish or cancel an existing booking cycle using the latest booking's creation time as a compatibility baseline. Completed cycles do not renew, and a legacy participant with no bookings requires an explicit invitation. Cancellation history remains usable rather than expiring permission on the canceled date. No bulk permission backfill is performed.

## Architecture and Deployment

Use Rails 8.1 with server-rendered views, Mongoid 9.1, and MongoDB 8.0. Ruby 4.0.9 is the stable runtime selected at scaffold creation. Test one MongoDB version, 8.0, to match the deployment target rather than maintaining a server-version compatibility matrix. Commit `Gemfile.lock` because this is an application, not a published gem.

Use Rails and Mongoid public APIs. Do not configure Active Record, Solid Queue, or another SQL-backed component. Active Job uses `delayed_job_mongoid` for a durable MongoDB queue. One shared worker handles named queues and recurring jobs with transactional duplicate protection, including calendar delivery and reconciliation. Retain failed jobs for controlled retry, log only IDs and error classes, and make operations idempotent for at-least-once delivery. No automated reminder workflow is enabled. See [Background Jobs](docs/background-jobs.md).

The Dockerfile explicitly selects runtime files, installs production dependencies, precompiles assets without live services, creates declared indexes at container startup, and runs Puma as a non-root user. The package check runs the built image against its own MongoDB 8.0 container and verifies first-user registration, compiled CSS, and liveness. CI reuses Docker build layers, runs the same smoke checks against the already built image without installing host application gems, and overlaps test database/image-library setup. Only superseded PR runs are canceled; default-branch checks are preserved. Application tests exercise real MongoDB persistence and concurrent bootstrap, reset, email-confirmation, and organizer-assignment claims. Deployment is intended for DigitalOcean App Platform with managed MongoDB and an HTTPS-terminating proxy, but no infrastructure or credentials are provisioned.

Production requires `MONGODB_URI` and a deployment-supplied `SECRET_KEY_BASE`. `APP_HOST` defaults to `localhost`; external deployments must set their own hostname. Production requests are host-restricted except for `/up`. Local test configuration uses a separate URI and a test-only database-name guard. Filter email, phone, passwords, and authentication tokens from logs. Email password recovery uses SMTP; participant notifications remain unimplemented.

Optional `newrelic_rpm` monitoring uses `NEW_RELIC_LICENSE_KEY` and `NEW_RELIC_APP_NAME` (default `Posebook`), following the Slack bots' environment-variable setup. Production starts the agent only with a license; development and tests disable it. Rails instrumentation reports unhandled failures, and explicit `NewRelic::Agent.notice_error` calls report handled SMTP and photo-storage failures without changing their existing responses or booking grants. Disable request/custom attributes, MongoDB query capture, exception messages, log forwarding, and browser injection; report exception classes and stack traces, not contacts, authentication links, profile fields, uploaded images, or private database values. No additional service credentials are required to build, test, or boot without monitoring.

## Delivery Scope

Implementation proceeds through focused PRs. SMS consent, phone verification, opt-outs, and invitation expiry are required before the corresponding public messaging functionality launches.

Single-use, 30-day personal program invitations support private links and optional transactional SMTP delivery. A specified email restricts acceptance to that address, including already-issued links. Without an email, they remain private bearer links that one chosen recipient can accept with any account. Blank-email invitations send no email even if delivery was selected. Registration fills in a specified email and makes it read-only; the server independently rejects mismatches without creating accounts, consuming tokens, or granting access. Wrong signed-in accounts must sign out and use the invited address, with the link retained across logout and login. Invitations store only token digests; the pending email outbox stores encrypted tokens. GET does not consume them. An explicit authenticated acceptance or invited registration atomically creates the account if needed, consumes the invitation, and adds a participant role without dropping existing membership roles. Failed registration or a concurrent losing claim leaves no orphan account. Participants see their assigned programs, not organizer controls or other participants' contacts. Automated booking notifications and reminders remain future work.

Invitation and booking transactions require a MongoDB replica set; development and CI use a single MongoDB 8.0 replica-set node. Production packaging verifies an actual transaction rollback. Explicit account and invitation emails use the shared delayed-job-backed `mailers` queue and an encrypted `EmailDelivery` outbox with ID-only arguments. Account challenges/profile changes and enqueueing commit atomically; optional invitation-email enqueue failure preserves the private link or explicit booking grant. Workers recheck original expiry, token validity, authorization, and booking entitlement, then send outside transactions. SMTP failures retry safely but acknowledgment crashes can duplicate delivery. Missing SMTP never pretends email was sent. Normal public registration remains closed; automated reminders remain unimplemented.

Version one excludes payments, attendee registration, and public participant directories.
